# ML Daily: design

Date: 2026-11-23. Status: draft for the owner's approval.

## 1. What this is and why

A small web app for steady AI/ML interview practice. The owner is preparing for AI and machine learning job interviews and assessments over months, not cramming for one date. Each day the app deals three new questions plus any earlier misses that are due for review. A "Do more" button deals another three, as often as the owner wants.

Every question has a correct answer and an explanation that is plain and simple but goes a bit deeper than the answer: why the answer is right, why the tempting wrong answers are wrong, and one note on how the idea shows up in real work or in an interview.

The app runs on GitHub Pages and must work well on a phone.

The owner does not write questions. All questions are written and checked by Claude.

## 2. Goals

- A bank that will grow to 2,000 to 3,000 questions covering the whole range of knowledge an ML role expects, including production and industry practice.
- **This round: the app plus the first 90 questions.** The owner will give the go-ahead for the full bank separately.
- Mixed formats: multiple choice, select all that apply, fill in the blank, worked numeric problems, and open "explain or design" questions.
- Mixed difficulty: easy, medium and hard.
- Every answer correct. Numeric answers are computed by running code, and every question is solved blind by a separate reviewer before it ships.
- Works on a phone (375 px wide and up) and on a laptop.

## 3. Non-goals

- No accounts, no server, no database. Progress lives in the browser.
- No questions written by the owner, and no in-app editor.
- No AI model called at runtime. Every question and answer is fixed and checked ahead of time.
- No browsing of the full bank with answers visible. It would spoil future days.
- No offline mode (service worker) in this round. A home screen icon (web manifest) is fine.

## 4. Anti-goals (would count as failure even if the app works)

- A wrong answer key, or an explanation that teaches something false.
- Trick questions, or questions that only test recall of trivia nobody asks in interviews.
- Multiple choice where the wrong options are obviously silly, so the right one can be picked without knowing the topic.
- A question that cannot be answered as worded, such as two defensible answers or missing information.
- Explanations that just restate the answer.
- Emojis in the interface (owner rule from other projects).

## 5. Constraints

- Static site on GitHub Pages. Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step for the site itself and no third-party libraries. This matches the owner's portfolio site.
- A Python script (standard library only) validates the question files and writes the single bank file the app loads.
- Repo commits use the owner's GitHub noreply address, never their personal email.
- The repo must be public for free GitHub Pages. Creating and pushing it needs the owner's go-ahead.
- Written text in the app and in the explanations follows the owner's style: plain, human, no em dashes, no semicolons.

## 6. Approaches considered

1. **Every question written one by one, then checked.** Writer agents write topic batches, worked answers are computed by running code, and a separate reviewer solves each question blind and critiques it. Best quality, highest cost. **Chosen by the owner.**
2. Mostly written questions plus number templates that regenerate worked problems with fresh numbers. Cheaper, but repetitive in feel with more generic explanations. Rejected.
3. Questions generated by an AI model at runtime. Needs an API key and a server or a key in the browser, costs per use, and answers cannot be checked ahead of time. Rejected.

The owner approved running many helper agents in parallel for writing and checking.

## 7. What the owner decided

- Target: ML engineer and applied AI/LLM engineer roles, early to mid level (0 to 3 years). Most questions sit at that level, and hard questions reach senior territory (system design, production tradeoffs). Data scientist and research roles are covered where they overlap, not targeted.
- Steady practice, three new questions a day plus review of misses.
- A repeatable "Do more" button that deals another three new questions each time.
- Bank of 2,000 to 3,000 questions in the end. First round is 90.
- Explanations: proper, simple, a bit in depth.
- Approach 1, with parallel agents.

## 9. Question formats and grading

| Format | What the owner does | Graded as right when |
|---|---|---|
| `mcq` | picks one of 4 options | the picked option is the key |
| `multi` | ticks any of 4 to 6 options | the ticked set exactly matches the key |
| `fill` | types a word or short phrase | it matches any accepted answer after normalizing (lower case, trimmed, inner spaces collapsed, hyphens and spaces treated alike) |
| `numeric` | types a number | it is within the stated tolerance of the key |
| `open` | writes or thinks through an answer, then reveals the model answer and key points | the owner marks it "Got it" (as opposed to "Partly" or "Missed") |

On a `fill` or `numeric` miss, a small "Count it as right" link lets the owner overrule the grader for a typo or a format slip. Options in `mcq` and `multi` are shown in a shuffled order, fixed per question so they don't move on review.

Numeric prompts always say the expected form, for example "as a decimal to 2 places". Answers like `75%` are read as 75 when the prompt asks for a percentage.

## 10. Question file format

Questions are authored in `content/<topic>.json`, one array per topic. One question:

```json
{
  "id": "eval-0007",
  "topic": "evaluation",
  "subtopic": "precision and recall",
  "format": "numeric",
  "difficulty": 2,
  "prompt": "A fraud model flags 50 transactions. 40 are really fraud. There were 80 fraud cases in total. What is the model's recall? Give it as a decimal to 2 places.",
  "answer": 0.5,
  "tolerance": 0.005,
  "check": "tp=40; fn=80-40; result = tp/(tp+fn)",
  "explanation": "Recall asks: of all the real fraud, how much did we catch? ...",
  "deeper": "In fraud work, a missed case usually costs more than a false alarm, so teams ...",
  "tags": ["classification", "fraud"]
}
```

Fields by format:
- `mcq`: `options` (4 strings), `answer` (index).
- `multi`: `options` (4 to 6), `answer` (list of indexes, at least one).
- `fill`: `answer` (main answer), `accept` (other accepted spellings).
- `numeric`: `answer`, `tolerance`, `check` (Python that sets `result`).
- `open`: `answer` (model answer), `points` (3 to 6 key points).
- All: `explanation` (required, 80 to 250 words), `deeper` (required, one short paragraph on real-world or interview use), `prompt` may contain code.
- Time-sensitive questions (regulation, a named protocol or tool) also carry `asof` (a date), so they can be found and refreshed later.

What the explanation must cover (my call): for `mcq` and `multi`, why the key is right and why each wrong option is wrong. For `fill`, `numeric` and `open`, why the answer is right and the most common mistake people make. The build checks that `deeper` is present, and the reviewer checks the rest.

Fast-changing facts (my call): questions test principles that stay true, not vendor APIs, model names, prices or version numbers. Well-established named ideas are fine (LoRA, DPO, the Model Context Protocol as a concept). Regulation questions stick to the main ideas of the EU AI Act and general privacy principles, and carry `asof`.

Text uses a small markup: blank line for a paragraph, `` `code` ``, fenced code blocks, `**bold**`, and `-` lists. Math is written in plain Unicode (σ, Σ, √, x², ŷ). No LaTeX.

**Code questions.** Questions that show NumPy, pandas or scikit-learn code (predict the output, spot the bug) carry the snippet in `check` too, and the check runs it to prove the key. These checks run in a separate virtual environment, `tools/.venv` (not committed), with NumPy, pandas and scikit-learn at pinned versions listed in `tools/requirements-check.txt`. The owner approved this install. PyTorch is not installed, so PyTorch questions stay at the concept level (what a line does, why a loop fails) and are checked by the blind reviewer only. Where an answer depends on library behaviour, the prompt names the major version (for example "pandas 2.x").

`tools/build.py` validates every file (required fields, unique ids, option counts, word counts, no em dashes or semicolons in prose, `check` result matches `answer` within tolerance) and writes `data/bank.json` without the `check` code. The build fails on any error.

## 11. How a question gets made (quality process)

1. **Blueprint.** For each topic I write a list of question slots: subtopic, format, difficulty, and the interview skill being tested. Slots are spread so no subtopic repeats inside a batch.
2. **Write.** A writer agent per topic writes the batch, runs every `check`, and runs `build.py` on its file.
3. **Blind solve.** A separate reviewer agent sees each question without the key, answers it, and only then compares. Any disagreement is a finding.
4. **Critique.** The same reviewer scores each question on: one defensible answer, wording clear, wrong options plausible, explanation correct and not just a restatement, difficulty label fair, interview-relevant. Anything below the bar is a finding.
5. **Fix and recheck.** The writer fixes findings. Questions that fail twice are dropped and replaced.
6. **Spot check.** I read a sample from every topic before shipping, plus every question that had a finding.

## 12. Daily flow

- **The day** turns over at local midnight.
- **Today's set** is: reviews due today (oldest first, at most 5, the rest roll over) followed by 3 new questions. It is saved, so reloading the page shows the same set and the same progress through it.
- **New questions** come in a fixed order: a seeded shuffle of the bank that rotates through topics and puts easier questions earlier on average. The next new question is the first one not yet seen.
- **Answering.** One question per screen. The owner answers and presses Check. The screen shows right or wrong, the correct answer, the explanation and the "Going deeper" note. Then Next.
- **End of set.** A summary (score, which topics) and a **Do more** button that deals 3 more new questions. It can be pressed again after each set, with no limit. When the bank runs out, the button says so and offers to redo past misses.
- **Review of misses.** A miss is due again the next day. A right answer on review pushes it out to 3 days, then 7, then 21, after which it counts as learned. A miss on review sends it back to 1 day. "Partly" on an open question counts as a miss.

- **Today**: the set, one question at a time, with a progress line.
- **Progress**: streak, total answered, accuracy, accuracy per topic (bars), questions learned, reviews due tomorrow.
- **Settings**: light or dark theme, export progress (a copy-and-paste code), import progress, reset.

Look: light by default on white, with a dark option, clean type, large tap targets, no emojis, simple inline SVG icons. These follow the owner's taste on the portfolio site. A clickable mockup is shown to the owner before the build is final.

## 14. Progress and moving between devices

Progress is stored in `localStorage` under one key, with a version number. It holds per-question history (date, right or wrong), review schedule, today's set, and settings. Export makes a compressed text code. Import on another device replaces that device's progress after a confirm. Questions that no longer exist in the bank are ignored.
